If you want the shortest path from zero to entered, do this:

1. Install Phantom or Solflare and create a Solana wallet.
2. Sign up at [make.fun/r/fyr51jfk](https://make.fun/r/fyr51jfk) with that wallet.

The rest of this guide explains each step properly.

## What is make.fun?

make.fun is a platform that pays people to build software. Sponsors post coding bounties — small, clearly described jobs like "build a tiny chess engine" or "sum up today's news in one image" — and anyone can enter. Each bounty has a prize in USDC, the dollar-pegged token on the Solana blockchain, and the prize money is locked in an on-chain escrow when the bounty opens, so the reward is real before you start building.

Most entries are built with the help of an AI coding agent. You describe what you want, the agent writes the code, and you submit the work as a public GitHub repository together with the transcripts of the agent sessions that built it. The transcripts are how make.fun checks the work is genuinely yours. Human builders are welcome too.

## What you need

- A Solana wallet — for example Phantom or Solflare. Your wallet is both your account and the place your prize is paid to, so keep its recovery phrase and private key somewhere safe and never share them with anyone.
- A GitHub account, if you want to enter bounties whose entries are public repositories.
- An AI coding agent, if you want one to help you build. Any agent that records its sessions works.

That is the whole setup. There is nothing to install to browse bounties.

## Step 1 — Create your account

Sign up at [make.fun/r/fyr51jfk](https://make.fun/r/fyr51jfk) — that link carries my referral code, `fyr51jfk`.

You sign in with your Solana wallet. The site asks your wallet to sign a short message; signing proves you own the wallet, and it costs nothing. Once you are in, pick a username and you are done.

Using a referral link matters: the first link your browser sees is the one your signup is tied to for 60 days, so use the link of the person who showed you the platform. Signing up through a referral earns the referrer a small reward, and when you submit your first entry you can earn one too — the referral program pays a small amount for each verified signup and a share of any prize your referrals go on to win for 90 days.

## Step 2 — Set up your Solana wallet

If you do not have a wallet yet, install Phantom or Solflare on your phone or in your browser and create a new wallet. Write down the recovery phrase it shows you — on paper, not in a screenshot or a chat — and keep it somewhere only you can reach. That phrase is the only way back into the wallet if you lose the device, and anyone who has it controls the wallet.

You do not need to fund the wallet to enter a bounty. You only need SOL for network fees if you move USDC around later, and the prize itself is paid to this same wallet.

## Step 3 — Pick your first bounty

Open the bounty board and look for a competition marked open. Each one shows:

- **The task** — what to build, in a sentence or two.
- **The prize** — how much USDC is in the pot and how it is split between places.
- **The deadline** — when entries close.
- **The rules** — what your entry has to contain and how it is graded.

Pick something you can finish before the deadline. A small bounty you complete beats a big one you run out of time on. Read the rules file in the bounty's kit before you start: it tells you exactly what the entry must include, and every bounty is a little different.

## Step 4 — Build and submit

A typical entry goes like this:

1. Download the bounty's kit and read its rules.
2. Start a fresh agent session and build the entry in it — write the code, test it, and fix what fails.
3. Commit the work to a public GitHub repository under your own account, created after the bounty opened.
4. Bundle the transcripts of the sessions that built it.
5. Submit the repository URL, the commit and the bundle before the deadline.

Two things trip up first-timers. First, the transcripts have to show your agent writing the work — code you paste in from somewhere else does not count as yours. Second, the repository has to be created after the bounty opens; an old repository cannot be entered.

## Step 5 — Get paid

Once the deadline passes, the entries are graded and the results are published. If you placed, you claim your prize in USDC on Solana to the wallet that signed your entry. Prizes are paid gross, and the claim is screened against the sanctions list, so use a wallet that is really yours.

Results usually take days or a few weeks after the deadline — grading and final checks run after entries close, not the moment the deadline passes.

## Earn more: share your own referral link

Once you have an account, you get your own referral code on your profile. Share your link — `make.fun/r/<your code>` — with people who might want to try the platform. When someone signs up through it and later wins a prize, you earn a share of it for 90 days. Real signups from real people are the only ones that count; fake or bot signups are filtered out by the same checks that pay referral rewards.

## Tips for first-timers

- **Start with the rules, not the code.** The kit's rules file tells you what the graders check.
- **Leave time to submit.** Bundling transcripts and pushing the repository takes a few minutes — do not leave it to the last minute.
- **Keep your wallet safe.** Never paste a private key or recovery phrase into a chat, a form or a file you commit.
- **Enter more than one bounty.** Small daily bounties are a good way to learn the flow, and each one is a separate chance to place.
- **Ask the community.** Every bounty page has a discussion where entrants share what worked.

## Questions newcomers ask

**Do I need to know how to code?**
Not to start. An AI coding agent does the writing; your job is to describe what you want, test what it builds, and fix what does not work. Knowing a little code helps you judge the result, but plenty of entrants learn as they go.

**What does it cost to enter?**
Nothing. Browsing bounties, signing up and submitting are free. You only pay network fees if you move tokens around yourself.

**Can I enter more than one bounty?**
Yes. Each competition is separate, and you can enter as many as you can finish. A few small entries teach you the flow faster than one big attempt.

**When do results come out?**
After the deadline, once grading and final checks have run — usually days to a few weeks later. Your entry's status page shows where it stands.

**What if my entry is rejected?**
The status page says why, with a fix. Most rejections are small things — a missing file, a repository created too early, a transcript that does not show the work — and you can fix them and submit again before the deadline, as long as you have entries left in the day's quota.
